# SolidWork-Design-of-a-3-DOF-Parallel
****Modeling, Dynamic Analysis, and Balancing Design of a 3-DOF Parallel Manipulator Platform****
Salman Alimardani, Mohammad Reza Gharib, Ali Delarami

Abstract
Parallel manipulator platforms are widely employed in flight simulators, robotic machining systems, and precision testing applications due to their superior stiffness, positioning accuracy, and load-carrying capacity. However, such systems often suffer from high actuator loads caused by gravitational imbalance and complex dynamic interactions among the mechanical components. This paper presents comprehensive modeling, kinematic and dynamic analyses, and the design of balancing systems for a three-degree-of-freedom (3-DOF) parallel manipulator platform. The geometric model was developed in SolidWorks and subsequently exported to ADAMS and Sim Mechanics environments for simulation and validation. Both forward and inverse kinematics were analyzed under multiple operating conditions, and numerical results were verified against analytical formulations. Dynamic behavior was investigated using sinusoidal motion inputs to evaluate actuator torque, power consumption, and reaction forces acting on the links and joints. Furthermore, a mechanical balancing system based on helical springs, as well as a pneumatic balancing alternative, were designed and optimized using MATLAB in order to reduce motor loads and improve overall energy efficiency. Simulation results indicate that the maximum actuator torques and reaction forces lie within the ranges of 10³–10⁴ N·m and 10³–10⁴ N, respectively. The proposed optimization significantly enhances mechanical stability and reduces the maximum stress experienced by the spring elements. The results demonstrate an integrated design workflow that combines multibody dynamics, optimization techniques, and CAD modeling for the development of high-performance parallel manipulator platforms.
